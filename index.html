Selected projects

Three stories, four chapters each

Each project follows the STAR structure: Situation, Task, Action and Result, plus the lesson I took away.

01 · 2023 – present

Global growth for the fine ceramics business in ASEAN and India

Result in one lineOpened a path to local production in Vietnam and a new market in India for ceramic components serving the semiconductor industry.

  • Ownership
  • Long-term thinking
  • Cross-stakeholder work
  1. S

    Situation

    Major customers of the fine ceramics business, which supplies components for semiconductor equipment, began shifting production to ASEAN and India, demanding lower prices and faster delivery. Competitors had already invested in the region, while our revenue there was still very small.

  2. T

    Task

    I was responsible for analyzing the market, our competitive position and our internal capability gaps, then designing and driving a restructuring program spanning three years of investment decisions and many stakeholders inside and outside the company.

  3. A

    Action

    I built a regular reporting and milestone framework with division leadership, then launched a "local-for-local" model:

    • Vietnam: ran a business simulation for producing at an existing factory, found local processing subcontractors and used trial production to verify quality.
    • India: opened a path into the market and built relationships with new customers through a joint exhibition.
    • Market: led two industry exhibitions in ASEAN and India.
    • People: designed and now run a global talent development program.
  4. R

    Result

    200+visitors at an exhibition in ASEAN, above target
    10/10inquiries at an exhibition in India turned into quotation requests
    2exhibitions in ASEAN and India that I led

    I completed the business case for production in Vietnam and found a local subcontractor that met our quality standards through trial production.

LessonReal exhibition data showed that brand awareness in the local market was still low, which is why I proposed revisiting our brand strategy for the region.

02 · 2017 – 2023

Kyocera's first commercial inkjet printer and a PCB design standard for A3 MFPs

Result in one lineKyocera's first commercial inkjet printer reached the market, and one PCB standard was reused across multiple models.

  • Ownership
  • Dive deep
  • Deliver results
  1. S

    Situation

    The printing business operated in a shrinking market. Kyocera needed to cut costs on its flagship A3 MFP line and, at the same time, create a new growth path with its first commercial inkjet printer, a high-risk project.

  2. T

    Task

    As technical leader, I was responsible for two things at once: automating PCB assembly for the A3 MFP, and establishing mass-production technology for the inkjet printer, with demanding waterproofing and reliability requirements.

  3. A

    Action

    • Analyzed the entire existing design and assembly process, then set a PCB design standard that allows automated assembly.
    • Set the technical standards for waterproofing and high reliability when bringing the inkjet printer boards into production.
    • Standardized FCT/ICT jigs suited to low-volume production from the design stage.
    • Visited key suppliers in China many times for hands-on technical guidance and training of local engineers.
  4. R

    Result

    #1commercial inkjet printer from Kyocera launched to market
    2patents registered

    The PCB standard I set was not used for a single model only. It became the foundation for improving the assembly process of later models.

LessonThe greatest value lies in the platform, not just one product. Standardize once so many models benefit.

03 · 2017 – 2023

Operational transformation with Hackathon, RFID and Python

Result in one lineCut operating costs at the Vietnam factory through a Hackathon-style program, then rolled the model out to the factory in China.

  • Driving change
  • Critical thinking
  • Data-driven decisions
  1. S

    Situation

    Two large programs ran at once: smart factory digitalization, and breaking a cost structure locked in by old habits. I was both the leader of the operational improvement activity and the RPA leader.

  2. T

    Task

    Significantly reduce annual costs at the Vietnam factory while keeping quality, build a success model that could be replicated company-wide, and digitalize the supply chain to end the lack of component traceability.

  3. A

    Action

    • Designed a change mechanism in the style of a Hackathon, led 2 teams in the department, and brought the ECRS principles and a "challenge conventional wisdom" mindset into every decision.
    • Decided to switch to low-silver (1%) lead-free solder despite quality concerns, and brought some outsourced processes in-house with a mobile mounting line.
    • Personally led negotiations and technical validation with outsourcing vendors and overseas factories.
    • Standardized supplier packaging into RFID-tagged returnable containers, combined with a new WMS to automate receiving inspection.
    • Wrote Python tools that automate everything from analyzing CSV data and drawing charts to sending reports.
  4. R

    Result

    2factories using the model, in Vietnam and China
    2Hackathon teams I led directly
    1%silver content of the new lead-free solder

    The RFID infrastructure made all electronic and electrical components traceable, and the RPA and Python tools cut the time spent on data processing and reporting significantly.

LessonTo make people change old habits, turn the change into a competition with clear numbers.
